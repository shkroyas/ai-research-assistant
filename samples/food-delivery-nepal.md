# Research brief: Analyze the food delivery market in Nepal

## Summary

Nepal's food delivery market, centered in Kathmandu, is a growing but competitive digital sector with several established and emerging platforms. Key players include Foodmandu (founded 2010), Bhoj (founded 2014), and Pathao, with international entrant Yango recently entering the market. The sector's growth is supported by increasing smartphone adoption and government-backed digital initiatives, though cash remains the dominant payment method and digital infrastructure challenges persist. The platform economy is increasingly important for youth employment and entrepreneurship in Nepal.

## Competitors

- **Foodmandu**: Founded in 2010 in Kathmandu, Foodmandu is a Series B-funded on-demand food ordering and delivery service and one of the most established players in Nepal's food delivery market. Investors include Team Ventures and True North Associates. [F1, F2, F4]
- **Pathao**: Pathao is an established food delivery platform in Nepal competing with Foodmandu and newer entrant Yango. It is actively used by restaurants in Kathmandu for online food orders. [F1, F3]

## Key observations

- The Nepal food delivery market is seeing increased competition with Yango's entry, directly challenging established platforms Pathao and Foodmandu and broadening consumer choices [F1].
- Foodmandu is the longest-running food delivery platform in Nepal, founded in 2010, and is the only Series B-funded company among tracked competitors, with investors including Team Ventures and True North Associates [F2, F4].
- Bhoj (previously Bhojdeals), founded in 2014 and based in Kathmandu, operates as a comprehensive food platform offering delivery, dine-in deals, restaurant reviews, and a digital wallet (BhojWallet), ranked first on Tracxn with a score of 56/100 [F2, F5].
- Cash remains the dominant payment method in Nepal, especially outside urban centers, though digital payments are gaining traction in Kathmandu driven by smartphone use and government financial inclusion programs [F6].
- Nepal faces structural challenges in its digital economy including low digital literacy, inadequate digital infrastructure, insufficient interoperability among service providers, and a lack of foreign investment [F8, F9].
- The ILO recognizes that ride-hailing, food and parcel delivery, and other digital platforms are supporting tourism, SMEs, digital payments, and youth employment in Nepal's growing digital economy [F10].
